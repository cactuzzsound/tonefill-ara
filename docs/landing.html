The tour

Every control, made for dialogue.

Three groups of knobs decide what ToneFill hears, how it builds the bed, and how it sits in the mix.

Detection

Decide what counts as clean

Set how strict ToneFill is about the material it keeps. Move a knob and it re-selects instantly.

  • Clean Level - how quiet a moment must be to count as room tone.
  • Voice Reject - push out voice, breaths and mouth noise.
  • Min Fill - keep only fragments long enough to stay consistent.

Structure

Shape how the bed is assembled

Control the stationarity and the joins, so the fill stays steady and the seams disappear.

  • Flatness - keep only the steadiest stretches, so the fill never jumps.
  • Bands - how finely the source is split for Spectral mode.
  • Crossfade - blend neighbouring chunks until the joins vanish.

Texture

Smooth it, then set the level

Diffuse the bed when it needs it, and hand the level to the mix or to Normalize.

  • Smoothness - widen the Enhance window for a softer, more diffuse bed.
  • Output - trim the final level, or let Normalize bake a target in LUFS or dBFS.

Engines

Three ways to choose the material

Pick the selector by ear, then A / B it against the source. When a clip is stubborn, switch engines instead of fighting the knobs.

  • Classic - the tuned gate stack.
  • Experimental - weighted per-frame scoring, the reliable default.
  • Spectral - harvests clean tone per frequency band across the whole take, so the low end stays solid even when no single moment is clean everywhere.

Enhance and EQ

A 5-band EQ, right on the bed

When the fragments will not blend, Enhance resynthesises them into a smooth bed - and a 5-band parametric EQ shapes the tone on an interactive response graph.

  • Enhance - PaulStretch resynthesis for a seamless, diffuse bed.
  • EQ - five flexible bands, drawn straight on the curve, taming any HF hiss.

Spectral, advanced

Place every band by hand

Open the spectrum editor to set each band's edges on a spectrogram of your source, or let Auto Analyze derive them from the clean tone. Total control over how the take is split.

  • Hand-placed edges - drag bands on the live spectrogram.
  • Auto Analyze - derive the band split from the clean room tone.

See it work

Every selection, on the waveform.

The waveform highlights exactly what ToneFill picked as clean - and in Spectral mode it shows the union across all bands, so you can see it harvesting material from the whole take. Drag your own regions in Manual, and open Expand for a large view with a timecode ruler.

FAQ

Good to know.

What is room tone, and why fill it?

Room tone is the quiet ambient sound of a space with no one speaking - the air of the room. When you cut a line, move a take or splice two recordings, that background drops out or jumps, and the edit becomes audible. Laying a matching bed under and between the dialogue hides the seams, so a cut scene sounds continuous.

How is this different from a room-tone library or a looped sample?

A library never quite matches the mic, the room or the noise floor of your scene, and a short loop repeats audibly. ToneFill builds the bed from your own clip, so the tone already matches, and it tiles seamlessly with no repeat you can hear.

What if the clip has almost no clean room tone?

Turn on Enhance - it resynthesises the sparse material into a smooth, longer bed. Spectral mode also helps: it pulls clean tone per frequency band from across the whole take, so it can build a solid bed even when no single moment is clean everywhere. In a pinch, use Manual to point it at the quietest spots yourself.

Can I fill one gap, or a whole scene?

Both. The fill rebuilds live and you can print it across any region, or Export a seamless WAV at any length. Turn on Full / WHOLE FILE to scan the entire take for clean material.

Does it remove noise or replace dialogue?

No - ToneFill is a fill tool, not a denoiser and not a dialogue replacer. It generates matching background to sit under and between your dialogue. Run your usual noise reduction before or after it as normal.

Auto or Manual - when do I use each?

Leave it on Auto and it finds the clean room tone for you. Switch to Manual when a take is tricky or you want a specific stretch of ambience - drag your own regions on the waveform, and open Expand for a large, zoomable view.

Any ARA2 host - Nuendo, Cubase, Studio One and Reaper - as a VST3 or AU, plus Pro Tools as an AAX AudioSuite plugin. macOS and Windows, 64-bit.
